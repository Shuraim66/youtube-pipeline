# Money Psychology Explained: 10 Mental Traps That Keep Smart People Broke

## What this video is
- **Audience:** working adults who earn a decent income but still feel stuck with money.
- **Promise:** your brain, not your intelligence, causes most money mistakes. Here are ten traps researchers have measured, and a simple fix for each.
- **Tone:** calm, clear, a little surprising, no hype. Not financial advice, and no get-rich claims.

## Opening hook (use this first)
Worrying about money can lower your thinking power about as much as losing a full night's sleep. In one study, the effect was similar to a 13-point drop in IQ. It's from a study published in the journal Science. Money problems aren't only about money. They change how we think. Here are ten mental traps researchers have measured, counting down to the one that matters most.

## The ten traps

### 10. The knowledge gap
- **Key fact:** only about one in three Americans aged 50 and over answered three basic money questions correctly. The questions covered interest, inflation and diversification.
- **Source:** Annamaria Lusardi and Olivia S. Mitchell, "Big Three" financial literacy questions (Wharton, 2015).
- **Fix:** learn the three basics, compound interest, inflation and diversification, before any advanced strategy.

### 9. Keeping up with the neighbours
- **Key fact:** in the Netherlands, people living next door to Postcode Lottery winners bought significantly more cars than other non-participants.
- **Source:** Kuhn, Kooreman, Soetevent and Kapteyn, *American Economic Review*, 2011.
- **Fix:** compare your spending to your own goals, not to the people around you.

### 8. The windfall illusion
- **Key fact:** in a study of about 35,000 Florida lottery winners, prizes of $50,000 to $150,000 only postponed bankruptcy. They didn't prevent it. Big winners were less likely to file in the first two years, but more likely to file three to five years after winning.
- **Source:** Hankins, Hoekstra and Skiba, *The Review of Economics and Statistics*, 2011.
- **Fix:** treat any windfall (bonus, inheritance, refund) as a plan, not a party. Decide where it goes before it arrives.

### 7. Living without a buffer
- **Key fact:** in the Federal Reserve's 2025 survey, 63% of US adults said they would cover a $400 emergency expense with cash or its equivalent, so more than a third would not.
- **Source:** Federal Reserve, *Economic Well-Being of U.S. Households in 2025* (published May 2026).
- **Fix:** build a small emergency buffer first. Even one month of essentials changes how you make every other decision.

### 6. Overconfidence
- **Key fact:** among 66,465 US households with brokerage accounts (1991–1996), those who traded most earned 11.4% a year, while the market returned 17.9%. The average household earned 16.4%.
- **Source:** Brad Barber and Terrance Odean, "Trading Is Hazardous to Your Wealth," *Journal of Finance*, 2000.
- **Fix:** trade less. For most people, a simple long-term plan beats constant activity.

### 5. Loss aversion
- **Key fact:** losses feel roughly twice as strong as equal gains. Researchers still debate how strong the effect is for small amounts.
- **Source:** Daniel Kahneman and Amos Tversky, prospect theory (1979), and Tversky and Kahneman (1992).
- **Fix:** before selling in a panic or holding a bad decision too long, ask: "Would I make this choice today if I were starting fresh?"

### 4. The default trap
- **Key fact:** at one large US company, 401(k) participation among new employees was 37% when they had to sign up themselves. Under automatic enrolment it was about 86%. Most people simply keep whatever is set for them.
- **Source:** Brigitte Madrian and Dennis Shea, "The Power of Suggestion," *Quarterly Journal of Economics*, 2001.
- **Fix:** make good choices the default. Automate saving on payday, so doing nothing means saving.

### 3. Present bias
- **Key fact:** when employees committed in advance to save part of their *future* pay raises, their average saving rate rose from 3.5% to 13.6% over 40 months.
- **Source:** Richard Thaler and Shlomo Benartzi, "Save More Tomorrow," *Journal of Political Economy*, 2004.
- **Fix:** don't fight your present self. Pre-commit your future self: decide now to save a share of your next raise.

### 2. Card blindness
- **Key fact:** in an experiment with tickets to a sold-out basketball game, people told they would pay by credit card were willing to pay more than twice as much, on average, as people told to pay cash.
- **Source:** Drazen Prelec and Duncan Simester, "Always Leave Home Without It," *Marketing Letters*, 2001.
- **Fix:** for spending you want to control, make paying feel real again: use a debit card, or check your total before every purchase.

### 1. The scarcity tax
- **Key fact:** when people were made to think about a hard money problem, lower-income participants scored significantly worse on thinking tests, an effect similar to a 13-point drop in IQ, or losing a full night's sleep. Indian sugarcane farmers scored higher after harvest, when money was less tight, than before it.
- **Source:** Mani, Mullainathan, Shafir and Zhao, "Poverty Impedes Cognitive Function," *Science*, 2013.
- **Fix:** make big money decisions when you're calm, never in a crisis, and build the buffer from trap 7 so fewer decisions happen under stress.

## Closing
None of these traps mean you're bad with money. They mean you're human. The people who do well usually aren't smarter. They've built systems that work even when their brain doesn't. Pick one fix from this list and set it up this week. Which trap do you fall into most?
