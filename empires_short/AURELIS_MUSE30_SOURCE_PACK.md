## Opening finding
Economists Priti Kalsi and Zachary Ward followed American families through census records from 1850 to 1940. Of the grandchildren of men in the top 1%, 93% did not reach the top 1% themselves.
- **Key fact:** of the grandchildren of men in the top 1%, 93% did not reach the top 1% themselves (census records 1850–1940).
- **Source:** Kalsi, P. and Ward, Z. (2025), "The Gilded Age and Beyond: The Persistence of Elite Wealth in American History," NBER working paper, via InvestmentNews.

## Myth-check
You may have heard that 70% of wealthy families lose their money by the second generation. That figure is usually credited to a Williams Group study, but family-wealth consultant James Grubman has challenged it in a 2022 paper titled "There is no 70% rule."
- **Key fact:** the 70% figure is an often-quoted claim that has been challenged; it is not a fact.
- **Sources:** Williams Group study as cited by InvestmentNews; Grubman (2022) as cited by InvestmentNews.
- **Rule:** never state 70% as fact, only as an often-quoted claim that has been challenged.

## The fix (habit 5)
- **Key fact:** the families that last don't rely on willpower. They rely on systems.
- **Fix:** make saving the default.
- **Lesson:** the families that last rely on systems, not willpower.
